# Camper Sauna Build Plan

## Context
22 ft 1970s camper. Back ~1/3 (~7.3 ft) cut off and replaced with a new aluminum-framed sauna/wet room/shower/toilet space. Frame is 1.5" × 1/8" folded aluminum angle stock (not forged), ~16" OC vertical stud spacing, attached to the camper's steel C-channel chassis rails with plastic washers for galvanic isolation. Room is approximately 7 × 7 × 7 ft. The rest of the camper (front 2/3) remains largely untouched for now.

---

## Part 1: Plan Outline

### Phase 1 — Sauna Build (Current Priority)

#### 1.1 Structural Mods

- **Hammock reinforcement** (2 adults + 10 gal water load case; ~900–1,150 lb design load):
  - Pair of full-height doubled verticals at each hammock anchor corner
  - 1/4" aluminum plate as wall skin at anchor zone (hook welded to plate)
  - Diagonal/knee brace from anchor node down to lower frame node, gusseted at both ends (small triangular plate at each brace end to spread weld load)
  - Flat strap X-bracing on hammock walls for anti-racking (secondary)
  - Keep hammock angle ~30–45° from horizontal (flatter = much higher anchor force)

- **Bench / floor layout:**
  - Front half of driver-side bench removed; floor finished and drain channel extended to driver wall
  - Back half of driver-side bench retained; passenger-side bench retained (minus exterior door zone)
  - A raised wedge/triangle section in the center of the back-half driver bench provides tall anchor points for rope recliner system; wedge slopes quickly back to bench height on either side

- **Rope recliner system:**
  - 10–15 ropes (6–8mm Dyneema/UHMWPE) strung between front wall and driver-side bench/wedge
  - Ropes closer to driver wall sit higher to support reclined body shape
  - Aluminum rod (~1/2–3/4" dia) bent into gentle sine-wave/U profiles, troughs welded to wall studs, peaks proud of wall → aluminum karabiners clip into peaks for easy height/tension adjustment (no knots)
  - Staggered peak rows (inner = more slack, outer = tighter) for fine tension adjustment
  - One fixed rigid bar at lumbar zone height as positional backstop
  - Mock up with bungee/temp rope before committing bench height and wedge dimensions

- **Workout ceiling bars:**
  - Sparse grid of round aluminum tube (~1.25–1.5" OD, ~1/8" wall)
  - Gusseted at frame nodes with small triangular plates
  - Installed before inner skin is welded

- **Mounting strategy (frame to camper chassis):**
  - One fixed datum mount group (round holes, fully clamped)
  - One guided mount group (slotted in fore-aft axis)
  - All other mounts: slotted/oversized holes + large fender washers + HDPE/EPDM isolation pads, snug not over-torqued
  - HDPE/neoprene strips at all aluminum-to-steel contact faces (galvanic isolation + compliance)

#### 1.2 Insulation

- Fill existing ~1.5" frame cavity with **rockwool** (preferred over polyiso/XPS in hot/wet/mobile zone — dimensionally stable, vapor-open, non-combustible)
- Add 1–2" exterior **polyiso** (foil-faced, preferred over XPS for better heat-margin and R/inch in warm service) on outside of frame
  - Source from roofing supply houses (ABC Supply, Beacon, SRS Distribution)
  - Use foam-safe adhesive beads + insulation washers/screws as hold-downs
  - Tape all seams with proper flashing/butyl tape (not cheap HVAC foil tape)

#### 1.3 Exterior Skin (Sauna Walls)

- **ACP panels already on hand** — use these for sauna exterior walls
- Mount ACP through foam to aluminum frame:
  - Mark frame lines on foam surface
  - Long screws/rivnuts through ACP + foam + into frame
  - **Compression sleeves/standoffs** (aluminum or nylon tube, length = foam thickness) at every fastener so foam is not crushed by clamping load
  - Large face washer under screw head on ACP exterior face
  - **Rivnut + machine screw** preferred (cleaner, more serviceable) vs long self-drillers
  - **One fixed hole per panel**; all other holes slotted in panel long-axis direction for thermal movement
  - Use mineral-core or solid aluminum ACP (not PE-core) given proximity to sauna temps
- Water sealing: butyl tape in all laps; taped foil-faced polyiso seams as secondary air/water layer; bonded-washer or gasketed fasteners; drain path at bottom edge; sealant only at penetrations (MS polymer or butyl, not random smears)

#### 1.4 Interior Skin (Welded Aluminum Shell)

- **Primary waterproofing strategy: welded aluminum monolithic shell** (not panel sealant system)
- Material: **5052-H32**, **0.080"** sweet spot for walls/ceiling; **0.080" tread plate** (diamond/5-bar, base thickness) for floor
- Aluminum tube/angle frame members: **5052** preferred; use **5356 filler wire**
- MIG with **spool gun**, **100% argon**, U-groove drive rolls, dedicated stainless brush for aluminum prep
- Welding rules:
  - **Continuous welds** at all water/vapor sealing seams (panel-to-panel, floor seams, floor-to-wall, corners, penetrations)
  - **Stitch welds** (short skip welds, alternating sides) to attach skin to framing (support only) — reduces warp
  - Tack heavily first, then backstep/skip sequence; no long continuous runs in one pass
  - Patches: copper backing bar behind blowthrough; short tacks working around hole; 5052 patch piece for larger holes; grind/flap-disc smooth (don't thin weld area); flood test after
- **Rounded interior corners** (bent radius preferred; welded+ground as fallback; or interior corner trim)
- Break all exposed edges (small radius with flap disc)
- **Brushed grain finish** option: Scotch-Brite belt/pad, one consistent direction, all panels matched
- **Flood test** before coating/closure; soap/air test for pinholes; fix and retest
- Sealant used only at penetrations and service interfaces, not as seam system

#### 1.5 Floor

- Floor currently slopes from front and back walls toward center
- Finish floor under front half of driver bench
- Extend drain channel to driver wall
- Verify slope drains toward channel (no pooling under rope recliner area)
- Tread plate aluminum over welded floor pan
- Sump pump accessible from bench hatch underside

#### 1.6 Waterfall / Water Feature

- Aluminum channel segments welded inside frame at an angle, stepping water zig-zag down the walls
- Circuit: starts near ceiling at driver side of interior door → runs perimeter with steady downhill grade → terminates at back side of exterior door → drops down square-tube gutter (1.5" channel welded to frame, webbing facing out = square tube profile) → floor drain channel
- Each trough segment: slight cross-fall to one corner so water reliably spills to next segment
- Lip/tab at each spill point to aim splash predictably
- Low-flow pump + strainer + overflow back to sump
- Manifold with ball valves to tune each leg
- Removable end caps or segments for cleaning/biofilm maintenance
- Noise control: vary drop height or add fine mesh/splash pad at impact points

#### 1.7 Horizontal Rails / Shelving System

- Key feature: **T-slot or drilled-hole aluminum extrusion** at 2–3 heights around room perimeter
- Allows repositionable bolted/clipped accessories: candles, lights, speakers, shelves, hooks — no new holes or vapor barrier penetrations
- Angles/channel welded with slight outward slope so water sheds off shelves
- Specific shelf locations TBD based on use; keep sparse for flexibility (orgy/group use)

#### 1.8 Ergonomic Accessories

- Start with **closed-cell EVA foam** cut to custom shapes (heat gun or knife; waterproof; easy to clean)
- Also noted for future consideration: inflatable wedge/ramp (Liberator-style), vacuum bead cushions, forearm pad sleeves on ropes, fine-mesh rope net insert for distributed support
- **All aluminum karabiners** (no stainless inside sauna to avoid galvanic issues with aluminum)

#### 1.9 Infinity Shower

- Most hardware already on hand
- Recirc-friendly water heater: **deferred purchase** (try existing water heater first)
- Rear hardware accessible from bench hatch underside
- Requires time; plumbing consumables only incremental cost
- Commission after floor/drain work complete

#### 1.10 Lighting

- Warm dimmable moisture-rated low-voltage LED strips in aluminum channels with diffusers (indirect perimeter + bench toe-kick)
- 1–2 sealed puck lights at shower controls/bench zone
- High CRI (95+) LEDs
- Filming-friendly: soft diffused off-axis key light, bounce fill from opposite side, fixtures out of direct eye line; dimmers per zone
- Controls: outside-zone dimmer + inside momentary scene switch

#### 1.11 Audio / AV

- **Waterproof portable speaker** (IPX7/IP67) on removable shelf rather than installed marine audio (simpler, avoids wiring/buzz/heat issues)
- Add small shelf or strap point so speaker doesn't slide
- **Monitor:** waterproof display wired through wall to PC in dry zone; mount on ceiling arm or front-wall arm at reclined eye level (eye level when reclined ~24–36" from floor — determine from mock-up before committing mount location)
- Monitor arm options: ceiling mount swinging down; floor post with sleeve socket welded to floor (removable); front wall mount at correct height

#### 1.12 Water System UI

- Located on front wall, driver side of interior door
- Controls for infinity shower + waterfall + sump

#### 1.13 Ventilation

- **No roof fan in sauna** — door/opening purge used for now
- Roof fan deferred to center-third (kitchen) roof replacement phase
- **Drain vent:** likely solvable with **air admittance valve (AAV)** rather than roof penetration (depends on toilet type chosen — composting/cassette = no drain vent needed; marine head with holding tank = tank vent needed)

#### 1.14 Door

- **Sliding vapor-proof door:** under consideration (DIY-doable)
  - Marine sliding hatch hardware + EPDM compression gasket + cam/lever latch pulling door into seal at close
  - Needs further research before finalizing door rough opening dimensions
  - Hinged door with compression gasket is simpler to seal if sliding proves too complex

#### 1.15 Cosmetics / Finish

- Consistent brushed grain direction on interior aluminum (one direction, matched across panels)
- Black or brushed anodized trim lines at transitions (pick one metal finish, don't mix)
- Concealed fastener caps where visible
- Rounded edge profiles everywhere touchable
- Removable teak or EVA duckboard over main standing zone
- Magnetic accessory rail option
- Heated anti-fog mirror pad
- Backlit niche shelf
- Quick-access labeled shutoffs (water + power)
- Service hatch that looks intentional

## Part 2: Reference — Key Topics and Figures

### Structural / Materials

- **Camper chassis:** 2 hot-rolled structural C-channel rails (~4" tall, ~1/8" thick, 5 ft apart), joined by 1.5" angle crossmembers at ~4 ft intervals — light-duty by modern standards
- **Sauna frame:** 1.5" × 1/8" folded aluminum angle (not forged/extruded structural), ~16" OC; bends easily over 5 ft span under body weight — use for enclosure, not primary load paths
- **5052-H32:** recommended alloy for sheet/plate in this build (marine-rated, good weldability/formability, corrosion resistant)
- **6061-T6:** stronger but less weld-friendly; common in extrusions; different behavior than sheet 5052
- **5356 filler wire:** correct filler for 5xxx-series aluminum MIG welding
- **Wrought vs cast aluminum:** wrought (rolled/extruded) is stronger/tougher; cast (poured) has more porosity/lower fatigue — all your sheet/angle/tube is wrought
- **Open C-channel vs square tube:** open channel twists/deflects easily; square tube far stiffer for same weight — use tube for structural members, angle for secondary framing
- **CTE aluminum:** ~23 ppm/°C; steel ~11–13 ppm/°C; rigid PVC trims ~50–70 ppm/°C; FRP panel (in-plane) ~12–25 ppm/°C; wood (along grain thermal) ~3–5 ppm/°C but moisture movement dominates in practice
- **Polyiso dimensional stability:** can show permanent dimensional change at elevated temps (>70°C); XPS starts permanent deformation above ~60°C; rockwool is most dimensionally stable of insulation types tested

### Thermal / Insulation

- **Sauna target temp:** ~120–140°F realistic with current aluminum framing (no interior thermal break); classic Finnish sauna (160–190°F) unlikely without added thermal break layer
- **Hydro Ban in-service temp (LATICRETE TDS 132):** latex membranes rated **-20°F to 280°F** continuous service when fully cured; application temp window much narrower (50–90°F substrate)
- **Polyiso R-value:** ~R-5.6 to R-6.5/inch (warm conditions); drops in cold
- **XPS R-value:** ~R-4.7 to R-5.0/inch; more consistent across temp range but lower ceiling
- **Rockwool R-value:** ~R-3.8 to R-4.3/inch; non-combustible to 2,150°F; dimensionally negligible movement under heat cycling; best choice for hot/wet/mobile cavities
- **Polyiso brands:** Rmax Thermasheath, Atlas EnergyShield, Hunter Xci, Johns Manville AP Foil — source from roofing supply houses
- **XPS brands:** Owens Corning FOAMULAR (pink), DuPont/DowDupont Styrofoam (blue), Kingspan GreenGuard (green) — available at big-box
- **Thermal bridging:** aluminum studs are major thermal bridges; dense aluminum = fast heat transfer between interior and exterior; exterior continuous foam helps but doesn't eliminate bridging

### Waterproofing / Membranes

- **Hydro Ban:** LATICRETE liquid rubber polymer; blue/green color; good vapor retarder when at proper film thickness but not a true continuous vapor barrier through joints/fasteners; apply in 2 coats; ~50 ft²/gal per coat
- **Vapor barrier vs vapor retarder:** foil tape + aluminum foil layer = true vapor barrier; Hydro Ban/FRP = vapor-resistant but not guaranteed continuous barrier without careful detailing
- **3-sided bond problem:** sealant bonded to both panel edges AND backing cannot stretch/move correctly — always use bond-breaker tape or backer rod to prevent sealant bonding to the back surface at joints
- **Backer rod / bond-breaker tape:** foam rod or tape placed behind joint gap so sealant bonds only to the two panel edges; allows sealant to deform in hourglass profile
- **Butyl tape:** preferred for lap joints, penetration seals, terminations; does not cure rigid; maintains flexibility under movement cycling
- **MS polymer sealant:** good flexible sealant for localized penetrations; paintable; compatible with most substrates
- **Hydro Ban coverage:** ~50 ft²/gal per coat (2 coats required); 5-gallon unit covers ~250 ft² finished
- **FRP panel:** vapor-resistant face but full FRP panel system (joints, trims, screws) is NOT a continuous vapor barrier

### FRP Panel System (Considered, Not Primary Choice)

- **Standard thin FRP:** ~0.090" (~3mm or less); needs **continuous backing** (not open stud bays) to prevent oil-canning and fastener stress; 4×8 sheets ~$65–$80 retail
- **H-channel joiner:** PVC or aluminum H-molding captures panel edges; ~$10–$35/stick; preferred over raw butt-joint-and-caulk
- **Expansion gaps:** ~1/8" between panels; required even with H-channel
- **FRP adhesive:** must be compatible/heat-rated; manufacturer-approved full-spread; not random blobs
- **Joint management:** expansion gap + bond-breaker + flexible sealant (2-sided bond only) + corner/termination trims = minimum system

### ACP (Aluminum Composite Panel)

- **PE-core ACP:** avoid near sauna envelope — plastic core degrades at elevated temps
- **Mineral-core or solid aluminum ACP:** required for this application
- **Mounting:** through foam to aluminum frame with compression sleeves + rivnuts + machine screws; one fixed hole per panel + slotted others; large face washers; movement joints critical even when insulated (sun alone causes several mm movement on 8 ft panel)
- **Compression sleeves:** aluminum or nylon tube cut to foam thickness; inserted at each fastener point so tightening clamps to sleeve not foam; without these, foam crushes and panels loosen/wave over time

### Hammock Load Math

- 2 adults + 10 gal water: ~450–575 lb static; × 2 dynamic factor = **~900–1,150 lb design load**
- Per-anchor tension formula: T = W / (2 × sin θ) where θ = rope angle from horizontal
- At 30°: ~1,000 lb per anchor; at 45°: ~700 lb per anchor; at 20°: ~1,460 lb (bad — keep angle generous)
- Anchor reinforcement: paired verticals + 1/4" plate + diagonal brace + gussets (triangular plates at brace ends)

### Ventilation

- Room volume ~343 ft³ (~7×7×7)
- ACH formula: ACH = (60 × CFM) / volume
- 200 CFM fan → 35 ACH; 400 CFM → 70 ACH
- Open door: wildly variable, 50–3,000+ CFM depending on wind/buoyancy
- Roof fan deferred to kitchen/center roof phase

### Plumbing / Drain

- **P-trap and drain venting:** required to keep trap sealed and prevent sewer gas entry; can use **air admittance valve (AAV)** instead of roof penetration in most cases — common in RV/marine
- **Composting/cassette toilet:** no drain vent needed; composting unit needs its own small dedicated exhaust
- **Sump pump:** accessible from bench hatch underside
- **Drain channel:** floor slopes to center currently; extending drain channel to driver wall required before rope recliner installation

### Waterfall Feature

- Uses aluminum channel stock welded inside frame at angles
- Zig-zag/cascade pattern around room perimeter
- Fed by low-flow pump; returns to sump via floor drain
- Gutter at exterior door side: 1.5" channel welded webbing-out (creates square tube profile)
- Doubles as interior mounting rail for accessories

### Rope Recliner

- Source: West Marine, REI, Sailrite, Defender, Amazon (verify UHMWPE)

### Aluminum Sheet Sourcing

- 5052-H32, 0.080" (walls/ceiling), 0.063–0.100" range discussed
- Ask for: mill finish, PVC film one side (scratch protection during fab)
- Source: local metal service centers, welding/fab shops (drops/remnants), trailer/boat fab shops

- Tread plate (floor): 5052 preferred over 3003 for this use; 0.080" base thickness
- Roof exterior skin: 0.040–0.063" typical

### Risk / Safety Notes

- Tree-on-camper fatalities: rare (~single digits/year nationally); driving fatality risk is orders of magnitude higher; hazard-tree avoidance gives more risk reduction per dollar than structural overbuild
- Wind load on 1 ft step face at 50 mph: ~50 lbf sustained (can 2× in gusts); continuous cyclic load at seam/joint is the real risk, not one-time structural failure
- Aluminum sauna interior: hot surfaces at sauna temps are uncomfortable/burn risk on contact; plan foam pads / EVA at frequent contact zones
- Galvanic corrosion: aluminum-to-steel contact + moisture = real degradation over time; use HDPE/neoprene isolation at all interfaces; anti-galvanic compound at mixed-metal fasteners
- Movement mount logic: one fixed datum + one guided axis + floating others; do NOT hard-lock all points or over-constrain the assembly
- Welding thin aluminum (0.020–0.025" original camper skin): extremely blow-through prone on any normal settings; 0.080" is ~4× thicker and far more manageable
